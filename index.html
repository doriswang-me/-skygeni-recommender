The Recommender Difference

Why seven signals beat any single model.

No single algorithm wins on its own. The hybrid combines complementary views of the same purchase history into one ranking — and outperforms every standalone signal by 2.5× or more.

Sequence

Markov chains, four orders deep

From "buyers of D" to "buyers of A→B→C→D", capture short and long sequential patterns with graceful fallback.

Co-purchase

Association rules with lift

Catch product pairs that travel together regardless of order. Lift filters out the popular-but-uninformative.

Lifecycle

Stage-aware popularity

What do most accounts buy at their 5th purchase? A stable baseline that ensures every recommendation has signal.

Explainable

Score decomposition per pick

Every recommendation comes with its seven component scores — no black box, defensible to sales and ops.

How it works

Three layers. One recommendation.

Each account's purchase history flows through sequence construction, multi-signal scoring, and cascading fusion — to produce a ranked list of next-best products.

01

Purchase sequence construction

Sort each account's transactions chronologically, deduplicate same-day records, and apply leave-last-out splitting: train on all-but-last, test on the final purchase.

Work Plan

Five phases. One pipeline.

Each phase produced a checkpoint deliverable reviewed with the SkyGeni sponsor before advancing. From data audit to handoff.

01
Discovery
Reviewed 33,589 events, 3,981 accounts, 634 products. Confirmed schema and evaluation expectations.
02
Pipeline
Built data loader, deduplication, sequence construction, and reusable Nth-order MarkovChain class (orders 1–4).
03
Signal Expansion
Added association rules (confidence + lift) and stage-aware popularity. Designed hybrid scorer with cascading fallback.
04
Evaluation
Ran leave-last-out across 3,510 eligible accounts. Computed Hit@K, MRR@K, NDCG@K. Per-signal ablation.
05
Handoff
Two notebooks, six-sheet Excel workbook (35,100 recommendation rows), and technical report with glossary.

Where it lands

Built for revenue teams.

Three deployment patterns where next-product prediction creates direct pipeline value.

Sales

Targeted cross-sell

Hand AEs a ranked next-product list per account, complete with score decomposition showing which signals drove each recommendation — defensible, transparent, actionable.

Marketing

Campaign sequencing

Identify which products to feature in nurture campaigns for accounts at each purchase stage. Stage-aware popularity aligns content to lifecycle position.

Strategy

Portfolio insights

Surface the strongest sequential patterns across your customer base. Identify gateway products that consistently lead to high-value follow-on purchases.

What's next

Three concrete next steps.

Prioritized extensions with order-of-magnitude impact and effort estimates from the official handoff report.

● Low effort · ~1–2 days

Weight tuning

A 3-fold validation grid over the WEIGHTS dictionary, using a held-out validation slice kept separate from the leave-last-out evaluation set. Risk-free first step because the evaluation harness already exists.

Expected gain: +1 to +3 points on Hit@5
● Medium effort · ~4–5 days

Time decay in Markov fit

Apply exponential decay (e.g., one-year half-life) to transition counts so 2020 purchases weigh less than 2026 ones. Implementation is a single multiplicative weight; tuning the half-life is where the time goes.

Expected gain: +2 to +5 points if catalog has churn
● High effort · 2+ weeks

Cohort ensembling

Cluster accounts by industry × size, fit one MarkovChain per cohort, and blend predictions by cohort similarity at inference. Highest ceiling, most moving parts.

Expected gain: +3 to +8 points on cold-start accounts

Beyond these three, the natural longer-horizon direction is a neural sequential model (GRU4Rec, SASRec) that can blend account- and product-level features with the sequence signal already captured here.
